03 / 12 — Foundations

FOUNDATIONS

Colour is three variables wearing a trench coat.

Every colour a designer works with — from a raw pigment to a dye lot to a screen swatch — can be described with three independent controls:

Hue — the colour family itself: red, teal, violet. This is what most people mean when they say "colour."

Saturation — how pure or muted the hue is. High saturation reads as bold and directional; low saturation reads as quiet, worn-in, tonal.

Value (Lightness) — how light or dark it sits. Value is what does the heavy lifting for contrast, mood, and photographing well.

A single hue can look like ten different garments depending on how you move these two dials — try it.

THE WHEEL

Twelve hues, one map.

Primary — red, yellow, blue. Can't be mixed from anything else; every other hue is built from these.

Secondary — orange, green, violet. Two primaries mixed in equal parts.

Tertiary — the six in-between hues (red-orange, yellow-green…), named for the primary and secondary they sit next to.

Split the wheel in half and you get warm (red through yellow — advances, energises, flatters most golden undertones) and cool (green through violet — recedes, calms, flatters most pink/blue undertones). Fashion collections often lead with one temperature and use the other as accent.

06 / 12 — Meaning

COLOUR PSYCHOLOGY

A colour is a message before it's a fabric.

Association isn't universal — it shifts by culture, context and era — but these are the reads a Western fashion market most commonly makes at first glance. Ask your students to challenge each one.

Red

Power, desire, urgency. The colour of a "look at me" garment — worn for confidence, warning, or celebration.

Blue

Trust, calm, corporate stability. The safest colour in a professional wardrobe — also the most-liked colour globally.

Yellow

Optimism, energy, caution. Hardest to wear near the face for some undertones; loudest colour on a rack.

Green

Growth, nature, and — increasingly — sustainability signalling in fashion marketing.

Purple

Luxury, creativity, royalty. Historically expensive to dye — the rarity became the meaning.

Pink

Softness or rebellion depending on saturation — millennial pink reads calm; hot pink reads defiant.

Black

Authority, mystery, timelessness. The default "safe" choice — and the hardest to make feel new.

White

Purity, minimalism, a blank canvas. Culture-dependent — mourning colour in parts of East Asia.

07 / 12 — Colour & the body

COLOUR ANALYSIS

The same red doesn't flatter every complexion.

Seasonal colour analysis groups flattering palettes by undertone (warm/gold vs cool/pink) and contrast level — not by literal season. It's a styling tool, not a rulebook: use it to explain why a colour reads differently on different people, not to limit anyone.

08 / 12 — Colour & the market

FORECASTING

Colour is decided years before it's worn.

Forecasting services study runway data, street style, economics and even mood — recession years trend toward comforting neutrals; optimistic years trend toward saturated brights. A "Colour of the Year" call from a forecasting agency ripples through fast fashion within a single season.

Ask students: what colour has dominated the racks around you this month — and what do they think it's signalling about the moment?

The forecasting pipeline

1

Trend agencies scout runway, art, culture & textiles — 18–24 months out.

2

Textile mills commit to dye lots based on forecast palettes.

3

Designers select from what mills produce — 6–12 months out.

4

Retail floors show the colour — the season you actually see it.

SESSION COMPLETE

Colour is a decision, not a default.
